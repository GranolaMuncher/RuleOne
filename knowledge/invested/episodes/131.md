# 131 · Jack Canfield on Eliminating Fear (Part 1)

**In one sentence:** Success coach Jack Canfield says to name your goal in exact numbers, ask repeatedly (rejection costs less than self-criticism), spend an hour a day on learning in place of TV, and use visualization to turn into action; Phil adds that investing is mostly a head game because you must go against the crowd.

## Key ideas
- **Investing is mostly psychology.** Phil says how you think about yourself, money and fear is 80% or more of the job, because average results come from doing what the crowd does. [01:00–03:30]
- **Be specific about the target.** Jack's "travel agent" idea: nobody can plan a trip without where, when, how long and how much. Define "rich" as a figure, and cost the lifestyle (his example: a yacht's slip, fuel and upkeep). Jack's own number was a pot that yields enough for the life he wants. [05:00–09:00]
- **Know what the money is for.** Freedom, impact, power? The answer shapes the plan. [07:00–08:30]
- **Asking and rejection.** If someone says no, your life is no worse than before you asked. The pain comes from the story you tell yourself. His *Chicken Soup* book was turned down by 144 publishers before the 145th said yes. Phil adds that Starbucks' founder was refused by hundreds of investors (Jack's figure, 242 banks, unverified). [08:00–11:00, 24:00]
- **Find the hour.** Three hours of TV a day is over 1,000 hours a year, about 25 forty-hour weeks. Cutting one hour a day gives about 365 hours of study, two months of full-time work. [12:00–15:00]
- **Preparation then insight.** Intense study followed by letting go can produce a breakthrough, a story Jack tells with lightning as a metaphor. Take the idea as motivation, not as evidence. [15:00–19:00]
- **Visualization creates tension.** Picture the goal in detail until your brain wants to close the gap, like a test drive that makes your old car feel worse. Jack says that makes you notice relevant opportunities and take action. Phil shares a written $1M-in-five-years goal from 1980–85 that he hit. This is anecdote, not proof. [19:00–25:00]
- **Comfort zone.** Writing his first $100,000 gift felt frightening, but stretching it changed his self-image. Danielle likens it to parting with her Whole Foods shares after the Amazon deal. [25:00–28:00]
- **Money follows values.** Phil says about 50 million Americans hand savings to managers who invest "amorally", and that most stock-market money comes from individuals whose values aren't in the choices. [27:00–30:00]

## How it maps to RuleOne
- This is the human side of the Radar and practice habit: carve out a fixed time each week for the screen and reading. The hour-a-day arithmetic is a good model.
- A written goal with a number mirrors the "minimum acceptable return" and "number you need" thinking in the earlier valuation episodes.
- Values screening is not a feature of the screen. If you want it, note it as a personal filter before you read a 10-K.

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Graham's "temperament, not intellect" theme (*The Intelligent Investor*, chapter 8 and the preface) supports Phil's "head game" claim.
- Munger's "learning machine" idea (Berkshire annual meeting talks, and *Poor Charlie's Almanack*) matches the hour-a-day habit. Buffett reportedly reads many hours a day, which Phil repeats elsewhere.

## Words to know
- **Structural tension**: the gap between where you are and the vividly imagined goal, which pushes you to act (Jack's term).
- **Amoral investing**: here, investing with no stated ethical view (Phil's usage), not "immoral".
