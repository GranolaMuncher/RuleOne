# 211 · Gillian Zoe Segal on Interviewing Warren Buffett

**In one sentence:** Gillian Zoe Segal, author of *Getting There*, tells how she got Buffett to take part and what the 30 high achievers in her book shared: they all failed, they knew their strengths, and they could communicate. Mostly life lessons, with a few useful for investors.

## Key ideas
- **Persistence, politely.** She asked for ten minutes at a charity lunch, was told to email his office, was blocked, then wrote a clear email and got the interview. It lasted about an hour. [02:00–08:00]
- **Buffett talked about life, not stocks.** Segal thinks his values and character attract the managers who work for him. Danielle adds that people and values are what drew her to value investing. [12:30–16:00]
- **Everybody fails.** She found that every person in the book had struggled for years. Success depends on how you react to obstacles. [16:00–18:00]
- **Don't dwell on omissions.** Buffett has made acts of omission; Segal says look at what you did right. Danielle ties this to Munger's comments about a missed investment, and Phil's guess (relayed by Danielle) is that the price never got low enough. See [210](210.md). [17:00–21:00]
- **Persevere or pivot?** No clean rule. Segal suggests relying on your own "monitor" and honest feedback from the world. [21:00–25:00]
- **Know your strengths and weaknesses.** Buffett quotes Tom Watson: smart in spots, and stay around those spots. Danielle maps this to the circle of competence. [25:00–26:30]
- **Unusual teachers.** Buffett's Dale Carnegie course, a lawyer who learned from poker and bridge, and several people who credited door-to-door sales. Selling teaches you to get knocked down and stay fresh. [26:30–30:00]
- **Communication matters.** Buffett says that without the Carnegie course he wouldn't have built the investor community that gathers in Omaha. Danielle notes value investing seems solitary, but leading people needs communication. [30:00–33:00]
- **A due-diligence aside.** A friend who allocates money to funds asks how a manager treats employees before investing. [32:30–33:30]
