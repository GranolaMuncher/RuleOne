# 402 · Managing Expectations

**In one sentence:** Phil and Danielle go deeper into Gretchen Rubin's Four Tendencies, test themselves and their spouses against them, and conclude that knowing how you respond to expectations helps you set up an investing practice you will keep.

## Key ideas
- **Why it matters for investing.** Phil says an investing practice depends on how you handle commitments, and that investors need to understand their own responses before choosing a method. [04:00–06:00]
- **The four types, recapped.** Upholders meet inner and outer expectations; Questioners need their own reasons and ask many questions; Obligers meet outer but not inner expectations; Rebels resist both. Rubin's second book, *The Four Tendencies*, has a quiz. [06:00–11:00]
- **Obligers need outside structure.** Set up accountability, such as a trainer or a buddy, so there is an outer expectation. [09:00–10:00]
- **The Obliger-Rebel mix.** Danielle describes someone who meets others' expectations until exhaustion, then drops everything. [11:00–14:00]
- **Rebels act from identity.** Rubin's advice is to ask "what kind of person am I?" Danielle says she wanted to become an investor like her father "but different". [16:00–19:00]
- **Watch what people do, not what they say.** Phil likens it to values: you learn your tendency from your behaviour. Rubin's book includes a questionnaire. [21:00–23:00]
- **Examples.** Phil says he and his wife are Questioners; Danielle gives cases of dropping an appointment when something more important arises. [22:00–31:00]
- **Promises differ in power.** Phil treats a promise to himself as binding, while Danielle says promises to herself don't work, which shows one approach doesn't fit all. [33:00–34:30]
- **Next step.** The hosts plan to apply it to financial goals for 2023. [31:00–34:00]

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Buffett's "inner scorecard" (Alice Schroeder's *The Snowball* ch. discussing his father's influence; also his shareholder letters) is a Questioner/Upholder-style, self-set standard. Verify the source before quoting.

## Words to know
- **Four Tendencies**: Upholder, Questioner, Obliger, Rebel.
- **Inner scorecard**: judging yourself by your own standards, not others' praise.
