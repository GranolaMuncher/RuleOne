# 471 · Pool Party Investing Practice

**In one sentence:** In a four-minute tidbit, Danielle suggests treating summer gatherings as investing practice: ask friends about what they know, because their expertise is background knowledge you don't have.

## Key ideas
- **Everyone knows something you don't.** Danielle says it is obvious and easy to forget that friends know different things from you. [01:00–02:00]
- **Keep a small flag in the back of your mind.** She watches for ideas that could be interesting from an investing view. [01:00–02:00]
- **Naming the reason opens the door.** Saying "that could be interesting from an investing perspective" makes it normal to ask more questions than usual, and people are glad to share. [02:00–03:00]
- **The result is mostly relationships.** She says practice is more about experience than results; deeper friendships came first. [02:00–03:00]
- **Background knowledge compounds.** The conversations may lead to something or nothing, but the knowledge may matter one day. [03:00]

## How it maps to RuleOne

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Munger's habit of reading and talking widely to build a latticework of models (Poor Charlie's Almanack; see also [473](473.md)).
- Fisher's "scuttlebutt" in *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits* is the formal version; see [474](474.md).

## Words to know
- **Investing practice**: Danielle's term for treating everyday curiosity as the habit that builds knowledge for later.

## Try this
At your next social event, ask one friend how their business or job makes money, who the competitors are and what could go wrong. Write down three lines afterwards, then search the industry on [/stocks/](/stocks/) to see whether any public companies fit.
