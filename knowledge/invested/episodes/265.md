# 265 · Interview with Bestselling Author Dan Heath

**In one sentence:** Dan Heath (author of *Upstream*) explains how to stop firefighting and fix the system that creates problems, and Danielle applies it to judging management: which companies prepared before the crisis and how they treat people during it.

## Key ideas
- **The upstream parable.** Rescuing children from a river forever versus walking upstream to stop whoever is throwing them in. Most organisations stay in reaction mode. [01:00–04:00]
- **Why we don't go upstream.** Heath's example is nurses who improvise around recurring problems (missing towels, lost baby security anklets), so the system never learns. Time pressure traps good people. [05:00–08:00]
- **Small habits help.** Short "safety huddles" let people share near misses, so isolated annoyances become visible patterns. [08:00–11:00]
- **Silos hide costs.** At Expedia, Heath says 58 of every 100 bookings led to a call and 20 million calls in 2012 were for itineraries, a roughly $100 million cost. Nobody's metrics covered "customers shouldn't need to call". (All figures are Heath's.) [11:00–16:00]
- **Prediction beats rescue.** LinkedIn found it could predict churn by about four weeks into a subscription, moved effort to onboarding and, per Heath, cut churn in half. [18:00–21:00]
- **Heroics signal failure.** Needing a hero often means the system failed earlier. Good prevention is quiet and gets less credit. [21:00–23:00]
- **Granular work at scale.** Chicago public schools identified ninth grade as the warning point and worked student by student; Heath says graduation rose from about 50% (as told). Getting close to the problem exposes flaws, such as harsh suspensions. [22:00–27:00]
- **Preparedness needs a day job.** Emergency plans on a shelf fail; practise the muscle (Trello's remote-for-all meeting rule). A pandemic was foreseeable, so boards and leaders should have had a view. Danielle recalls the Buffett tide-goes-out idea. [27:00–31:00]
- **Promotion and prevention together.** Research on the 2007–09 recession found the best companies toggled between growth and caution selectively (an office-supply store closed weak shops but backed profitable services), rather than ignoring bad news or only cutting. [31:00–34:00]
- **Employees as a diagnostic.** Watch whether executives cut their own pay before layoffs, offer sick leave and speak honestly. Treating staff as a cost centre shows in a crisis. [34:00–40:00]

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Buffett's line about the tide going out and seeing who was swimming naked is from the 2001 Berkshire letter.
- Munger's inversion ("invert, always invert") is the same idea as asking how a problem is created before treating it.
- Management's honesty with shareholders is a theme of Buffett's letters (for example the 1990s "Owner's Manual").

## Words to know
- **Upstream**: acting on causes before problems occur.
- **Silo**: a team measured only on its own goals, so shared problems belong to nobody.
- **Churn**: the share of customers who leave in a period.
