# 405 · Free Solo

**In one sentence:** Phil and Danielle debate goals, promises and failure, and use the climbing film *Free Solo* to argue that confidence in investing has to be earned by repeated preparation, not by psyching yourself up.

## Key ideas
- **Promise vs. resolution.** Phil makes few resolutions because he does not want to set himself up to fail; he makes a "promise" only on things he truly means to do (the $2 million phone app). Danielle says people differ, and a new-year goal helps some. Know what you respond to. [01:00–05:30]
- **Is feeling bad useful?** Phil says feeling bad about a mistake is how you learn. Danielle says that is a different question for adults picking a goal than for children. [05:30–10:00]
- **The hero's journey.** Phil cites Joseph Campbell: every hero passes through doubt, fear and "I'm over my head". If you avoid that stage you are not on the journey. Few goals deserve that treatment, and financial independence is one. [11:00–15:00]
- **Vows vs. goals.** Danielle notes that marriage doesn't feel like an expectation to her but a settled choice, "already in the past". She wonders if a deep commitment to becoming an investor could feel the same, with a daily practice carrying it. [17:00–23:00]
- **Burning the ships.** With no retreat, the only options are to win or die. Phil uses it as a picture of full commitment. [23:00–24:00]
- **Free solo.** In the film Alex Honnold climbs without ropes. Danielle describes the brain scan showing little fear response, but says the real edge is preparation: he rehearses each move in his head, on paper and roped, until the confidence is real. [24:00–31:00]
- **Doubt kills.** Phil's reading is that once you are committed and prepared, the doubts go away; the moment you doubt mid-climb, you fall. Honnold turned back on one attempt because it did not feel right. [26:00–28:30]
- **Investing without a rope.** A second climber, trusting Honnold, froze on the wall with no preparation. Danielle uses that as a picture of investing real money without having done the repetitions. [30:00–33:00]
- **First be sure you're right.** Phil quotes Davy Crockett's motto. In investing it means being an expert in the business and staying away from what you don't know. [33:00–34:30]
- **Munger on a wonderful business with a con artist.** Phil and Danielle disagree on Munger's preference for a wonderful business run by a poor manager, then clarify that "idiot-run" means weak but honest, not fraudulent. They agree that early-stage investors back people. [34:00–36:00]

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Buffett's "circle of competence" (1996 Berkshire letter) is the "stay away from what you don't know" rule.
- Munger's preference for a wonderful business over a fair one runs through the Berkshire talks; the management filter is the fourth Munger step, so treat the con-artist example as a thought experiment.
- Graham's *Intelligent Investor* (ch. 8) on temperament: the investor's chief problem is himself.

## Words to know
- **Hero's journey**: Campbell's pattern in which a hero passes through doubt before success.
- **Top-roping**: climbing with a rope from above, safe enough to rehearse moves.
- **Practice**: a repeated, scheduled routine that builds skill and confidence.
