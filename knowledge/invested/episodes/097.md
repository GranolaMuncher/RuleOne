# 097 · JJ Virgin and the Miracle Mindset (Part 2)

**In one sentence:** The second half of the JJ Virgin interview ties mindset to investing: anchor your effort to a specific "big why", lean into fear to build resilience, and think of Rule #1 as an anti-fragile strategy that gains when others panic.

## Key ideas
- **The common blockers.** No time, no knowledge, fear. JJ's habit is to listen for "so I don't have to" at the end of those sentences, which means taking responsibility instead. [01:00–03:00]
- **A big why, made specific.** Attach the habit to a purpose, then be as clear as possible about what that life looks like. "I need more money" is vague. Ask why until you reach the emotional reason. [03:00–08:00]
- **Anchoring.** The anchor is the stretch goal that keeps you moving. For JJ it was saving her son's life, and she pictured his future. [05:00–07:30]
- **Seven attributes of the miracle mindset.** Resilience, taking imperfect action, abundance thinking, living in the present, courage, collaboration and forgiveness. [08:00–11:00, 24:00–33:00]
- **Small steps build resilience.** Stepping out of your comfort zone, for example from a 401(k) default into managing your own investments, builds competence, and each success widens the zone. A story about an axe hitting an oak, sand and water illustrates a nervous system that returns to balance faster. [10:00–15:00]
- **Anti-fragility.** Taleb's term for something that benefits from shocks. A fragile strategy needs everything to go well. Phil says Buffett-style investing is anti-fragile because it needs a market panic to buy wonderful companies on sale, so black swans help. [15:30–19:00]
- **Lean in.** The average person runs from scary events. Phil gives special-operations selection as an example of looking for people who step in. JJ says if it's always safe you're not stretching, and Phil adds that playing safe has its own risk. [18:00–22:00]
- **Family backgrounds.** Phil contrasts his risk-averse father with his entrepreneurial mother, and Danielle is the risk-averse lawyer who is learning. The aim is small steps, not special-ops training. [20:00–24:00]
- **Active forgiveness.** Unresolved grudges drain energy. JJ describes a structured process: air the grievance, step into the other person's shoes, forgive, then check that no emotional charge remains. Self-forgiveness is the hardest. [24:00–31:30]
- **Where to start.** Measure yourself on the seven attributes, then begin with gratitude (three things in the morning and three wins at night), plus a short exercise of leaning into fear for about 90 seconds. Phil says that mastering fear is what lets you buy when others panic. [31:00–36:00]

## How it maps to RuleOne
- The screen can find the cheap, high-quality business during a panic, but a person has to place the order. The mindset work covers that step.
- Setting a specific "big why" (a number, a date and a purpose) is a useful first entry in a holdings note on /holdings/.
- Start small: a few shares of a business you understand builds the experience without a big risk.

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Mr. Market (Graham, *The Intelligent Investor*, chapter 8) is the same idea of using other people's panic. Buffett's 2008 *New York Times* piece "Buy American. I Am." is a public example of buying in fear.
- Taleb's *Antifragile* (2012) and *The Black Swan* (2007) are the sources of the terms. Phil's reading of Buffett as anti-fragile is his own interpretation.
- Munger, in the 2017 Daily Journal meeting (see [098](098.md)), says to expect big declines and be able to handle them calmly.

## Words to know
- **Anchor / big why**: the specific purpose that keeps you going through difficulty.
- **Anti-fragile**: gaining from volatility and shocks, not merely surviving them (Taleb).
- **Black swan**: a rare, unexpected event with a large impact.
- **Abundance thinking**: being open to possibilities beyond your current situation.

## Try this
Write your big why in two sentences, asking "why?" three times, so that it names a specific life, not just a dollar figure. Keep it with your /holdings/ notes and read it before you next consider selling in a downturn.
