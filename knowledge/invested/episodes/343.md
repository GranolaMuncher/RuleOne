# 343 · The Investment Checklist Pyramid

**In one sentence:** Phil describes three layers of research (Munger's 4, Ackman's 8, then the long Rule #1 checklist) using Sprouts as a worked example, and says to find a rational partner to test ideas against.

## Key ideas
- **A pyramid of three checklists.** Top: Munger's four questions (understand it, moat, management, price). Middle: Bill Ackman's eight principles. Base: the long Rule #1 checklist (Phil's "millions of questions"), which takes about 100 hours and produces a ~20-page document. You only go down a level if the company passes the one above. [02:00–04:00, 09:00–11:00, 30:30–31:30]
- **Layer 1 happens in your head, in minutes.** Sprouts Farmers Market: can I understand a grocery store? Grocery is a commodity business, so the moat question matters most. Phil then looks at ten years of numbers on a data tool: high margins versus peers suggest a moat, and little debt and good returns on equity and invested capital suggest able management. Total time about five minutes. [03:00–08:30]
- **Two piles.** A company either goes to "too hard" (can't understand) or is dropped (numbers don't support a moat). Anything left earns the next layer. [06:00–07:00]
- **Layer 2: Ackman's eight, still mostly in your head.** Simple and understandable; dominant market position; strong free cash flow; barriers to entry; high returns on capital; low extrinsic risk; a strong balance sheet; and excellent management. For Sprouts, some boxes are checked quickly (free cash flow, returns, balance sheet) and some stay unknown (dominance, barriers, management). Unknown means "park and dig", not "reject". [08:30–16:00]
- **Dominance is relative.** Sprouts is small against Kroger and Whole Foods but strong in its niche. [12:00–14:00]
- **Teaching sharpens the checklist.** The process became systematic in the last decade, partly because teaching forces you to examine your assumptions. [16:00–17:30]
- **Get a trusted, rational partner.** Great investors have a small trusted group (Pabrai and Spier famously have each other, no analysts). Ackman runs about $10B with five analysts. A partner catches errors in your numbers and assumptions. Danielle notes a non-investor partner can be a useful outsider but also a distraction. [17:00–23:00]
- **Be rational, not emotional.** A partner who is emotional isn't helpful; "excited" isn't analysis. Phil describes Munger as more rational than Buffett and ties Buffett's selling-off-a-Nebraska-town story (in Schroeder's *The Snowball*) to concern about how he's perceived. Buffett and Munger vote their own values (tobacco and gambling avoided). [23:00–26:30]
- **Love the business, within reason.** "Love" is one of the long checklist's points. Danielle says falling for companies is the best part of investing. Phil warns that if the company changes it's like heartbreak. Price is a separate analysis from company quality. [27:00–30:30]

## Words to know
- **Checklist pyramid**: Munger's 4, then Ackman's 8, then the full Rule #1 list.
- **Too-hard pile**: companies you set aside because you can't understand them.
- **Extrinsic risk**: danger from outside the business, like regulation.
