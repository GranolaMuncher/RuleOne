# 336 · Investing Checklist Takeaways

**In one sentence:** Danielle, solo, gives three takeaways for building her own checklist from her father's: make it personal and aimed at your own blind spots, treat it as due diligence as well as a reminder, and keep it short with short answers.

## Key ideas
- **Why it exists.** The checklist's job is to reduce risk so you don't permanently lose capital. Nobody grades it. [04:00–05:30]
- **Takeaway 1: it's personal.** Wording that is clear to Phil can mean nothing to Danielle. A list should expose the holes in your own thinking, so you need to write items that force you to fill them. [05:00–07:00]
- **"Of course" is a warning sign.** When she thinks "of course customers love it" she knows she's being lazy and needs evidence. [06:00–07:00]
- **Takeaway 2: it's also due diligence.** It does double duty, a memory aid and a research prompt. When you don't know which questions to ask about a company, the list supplies them (for example, check ROIC). [07:00–09:00]
- **Adapt the items, not the idea.** Phil's "my kids would be proud" becomes "my friends would be proud" if you have no kids. Its job is to stop you skipping a basic requirement. [08:00–09:00]
- **Takeaway 3: keep it simple.** Long lists don't get started or finished. Items that demand one sentence, a yes/no or a list of three work because you either know it or you don't. [09:00–13:00]
- **Brevity also screens companies.** If you can't say what a company does in one sentence, either you don't understand it or the company is scattered. [11:00–12:00]
- **What's next.** The last section is story and inversion: ask "what if that's not true?" about everything you wrote. [13:00–14:00]

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Munger's habit of checklists and mental models: "Poor Charlie's Almanack" (the talk on the psychology of human misjudgment) lists biases a checklist guards against.
- Atul Gawande's *The Checklist Manifesto* is the standard outside reference, though the episode doesn't mention it.

## Words to know
- **Due diligence**: the research that confirms the facts behind your investment story.
- **ROIC**: return on invested capital, one of the Big Five numbers (mentioned in passing).
