# 404 · In The Moment

**In one sentence:** Phil updates the Google story with two new risks (a Justice Department monopoly suit and ChatGPT as a threat to search ads), then he and Danielle drift into the tension between living in the moment and planning for the future.

## Key ideas
- **Regulatory risk, updated.** The US Justice Department has filed a monopoly case against Google. Phil doubts a forced sale of the ad business, since any buyer would become a monopolist, and guesses a spin-off is the most that happens. He sees it as no obstacle to being a shareholder (his view, not a forecast). [02:00–04:00]
- **Europe.** Rules against targeting by race, religion or politics may force Google to rewrite algorithms in Europe, which Phil thinks is not a big deal. [03:30–04:00]
- **A new kind of risk: technology.** ChatGPT (OpenAI, now backed by Microsoft, reportedly with about $10 billion planned) can give a written answer instead of a list of links. Phil says search ads are about 67% of Google's revenue. [04:00–06:00]
- **Google's dilemma.** Its own matching product would hurt the keyword-ad business, so it cannot respond freely. Phil says Google has historically bought its way out of threats, but cannot buy OpenAI. He quotes Google as being on "red alert". [05:00–10:30]
- **Source problem.** When they asked the tool where it got its facts, it pointed to a paid data abstract, so Phil notes it can sweep up material behind paywalls. [06:00–07:30]
- **Watch, don't panic.** Phil thinks the threat is a few years away and says the plan is to keep watching and exit if warranted. This is the risk they had "not on the radar" because they assumed the best engineers would stay ahead. [07:00–11:00]
- **Is it a monopoly?** The product is free and advertisers can use Facebook and others, so Phil struggles to see the harm to the public. Danielle notes monopoly cases are ultimately public-policy decisions that become precedent. [12:00–16:00]
- **Resolutions vs. expectations.** Danielle makes none, because she responds badly to expectations (the Gretchen Rubin framework) and does not want to set herself up to fail. Phil has "a promise" instead: about $2 million to build a phone toolbox app. [16:00–20:00]
- **Be here now vs. prepare.** Phil says some people who went deep into "be here now" are struggling at his age with nothing saved. Balancing the present against planning is a lifelong problem. [18:00–24:00]
- **Equanimity.** The axe-in-an-oak versus axe-in-a-pond image: an enlightened mind feels the impact more but returns to calm quickly. [28:00–30:00]

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Buffett's frequent warning about technology disruption of a "castle" (for example the newspaper business in his 1990s–2000s letters) is the same shape as the search-ads risk. Check the letter year before quoting.
- Munger's "invert, always invert": ask what could break the moat, as Phil does here.

## Words to know
- **Regulatory risk**: the chance that law or enforcement damages a business.
- **Technological risk**: a new technology that undermines a company's main profit source.
- **Equanimity**: staying even-tempered through good and bad events.
