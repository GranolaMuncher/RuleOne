**In one sentence:** This is a replay of [310](310.md) (March 2021), with nothing new added; the one thing to take is Phil's test of wanting a business as predictable as a rental house.

## Key ideas
- **Rerun of 310.** The audio is the earlier episode, with its market comments of the time (Wilshire-to-GDP at about 216%, the Fed's low-rate stance, a hot housing market). Treat those figures as dated to early 2021. [00:00–10:00]
- **Reading widely makes ideas.** A nuclear company's report prompted Phil's thoughts on energy; he notes such a company argues its own case. [10:00–19:00]
- **Don't bet on the far future.** Phil's example: IBM in the 1950s versus railroads, and Buffett meeting Bezos in the 1990s but declining to call it a sure thing. Buffett later said he should have bought Amazon once it had proven itself. [26:00–32:00]
- **The rental-house standard.** Phil wants a company he's as sure of as a rental house bought at a 10% yield: more rent in ten years, a location moat, and inflation on his side. If the moat is in doubt (oil and gas), "cheap" may be cheap for a reason. [32:00–35:00]
