# 251 · Checklist

**In one sentence:** Phil and Danielle ask what makes a good investing checklist (short, precise, personal, usable under stress), and start thinking about what the first checklist should cover: how an idea reached you.

## Key ideas
- **The pitch, restated.** Buy $10 bills for $5. They go cheap because fear shortens everyone's horizon; Graham's way is to buy fear and sell greed. [00:00–03:00]
- **Do the masters use checklists?** Phil says Buffett and Munger have never shown one. Danielle finds a "prior to pulling the trigger" checklist in *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (the editor's description; the book gives little sourcing), and a 148-page compilation of other investors' checklists on a website. Munger's four filters are about as close to a published checklist as it gets. [03:00–10:00]
- **Atul Gawande's *The Checklist Manifesto*.** Good checklists are short and cover the critical steps; pilots' checklists are nested by phase (start, takeoff, shutdown) rather than one long list. Quoted criteria: precise, efficient, usable under difficult circumstances, not trying to spell out everything. [10:00–13:30]
- **Too long is as bad as too short.** An over-long list never gets used. Mohnish Pabrai's checklist has 100+ items built from past mistakes; Phil admits that he has stopped opening his own lists when they got long. [15:00–17:00]
- **Know thyself.** A checklist is a personal tool: someone who loves detail needs more items, others would be put off. Different wording prompts different people. [16:00–18:00]
- **Nested checklists.** Phil's proposal: first checklist = "am I starting on solid ground?", meaning how did I find this company. [18:00–20:00]
- **How did this reach me?** Phil: it should come from a guru's buying, or from your passion, work or spending. Danielle disagrees about rejecting ideas that arrive another way: she logs every one, including rejected ones, with the source (e.g. "founded in a Guardian article, full stop"). [20:00–25:00]
- **Cloning.** Phil: most people start by copying a good investor, and Buffett and Munger say not to reinvent the wheel; an inexperienced investor probably can't hold a big position on their own research alone. [25:00–27:00]
- **Prescriptive or descriptive?** They debate whether a checklist tells you what to do or has blanks to fill in. Unresolved. [26:00–28:30]

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Munger's four filters (understand, moat, management, price) as a minimal checklist; *Poor Charlie's Almanack* (3rd ed.) for his pre-trade check, per the editor.
- Munger's "inversion" and Gawande's book (*The Checklist Manifesto*, 2009) both push you to list how things go wrong.

## Words to know
- **Checklist**: a short list of critical checks done before a decision.
- **Nested checklist**: one list per stage so each stays short.
- **Cloning**: using respected investors' buys as a starting tip.
