## Key ideas
- **Intro (new).** From Munger's Daily Journal talk (see [357](357.md)), Danielle quotes that decisions often mean choosing the least bad option. Munger lists his own holdings (Berkshire, Costco, Chinese stocks via Li Lu, some Daily Journal, apartment houses) and calls it "okay", not perfect. The least bad option can still be an excellent one. [00:00–02:30]
- **Why the rerun.** She picks the McKelvey interview because it is about deciding without knowing the future, which fits the war in Ukraine and market swings. [02:00–03:00]
- **The rerun.** It's the Jim McKelvey interview on *The Innovation Stack*: companies that win by doing 10–20 things differently, a CEO who succeeds because they are prevented from copying, and why most companies should copy what works. See [303](303.md) for the full notes. [03:00–52:00]

## How it maps to RuleOne
- The "least bad option" idea is the practical side of a **margin of safety**: you rarely get a perfect choice, so pick the one with the best protection if you're wrong.
