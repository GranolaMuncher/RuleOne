# 234 · Veronica Dagher on the Secrets of Successful Women

**In one sentence:** Danielle talks with Wall Street Journal reporter Veronica Dagher about her book *Resilience* and what setbacks teach, and a part of the conversation covers why few women are visible as professional investors.

## Key ideas
- **Resilience is the common thread.** Dagher (host of the *Secrets of Wealthy Women* podcast and author of *Resilience: How 20 Ambitious Women Used Obstacles to Fuel Their Success*) says the women she profiled looked as if everything had gone their way, yet each had overcome career, money or personal setbacks. [02:00–05:00]
- **Nobody really knows what they're doing.** Danielle and Dagher say even successful people are partly guessing and getting lucky with timing. For an investor this is a reminder that confidence should come from preparation, not from believing others have it figured out. [05:00–06:30]
- **Low baselines reduce fear.** Barbara Corcoran came close to failing seven times, by her account, and Dagher thinks growing up with little made losing everything less frightening. Danielle links it to professional investors whose fear of losing money and status warps their decisions. [06:30–11:00]
- **You are more than your account.** Dagher: you are not your bank balance, your business or your house. Holding that view makes it easier to act with courage. [10:00–11:30]
- **Recover one step at a time.** Melissa Francis, after being fired, drove up and down the East Coast dropping off her resume. The advice Dagher quotes from her is to set a new, bigger goal and take one step, not to map the whole route back. [11:00–14:30]
- **Pivot without quitting.** Mary Higgins Clark had many short stories rejected, moved to novels and then found success. Danielle asks which criticism to heed; Dagher says gut instinct matters about your core ability, while advice about strategy may deserve a pivot. [14:30–18:00]
- **Few visible women investors.** Dagher says there are not many high-profile women investors to feature, partly because investors may fear failures will make them look bad, partly because bank PR departments control what employees say, and partly because standing out when you are already the only woman in the room is uncomfortable. [18:00–25:00]
- **Role models matter.** Danielle says her investing role models were all men (family and the Buffett and Munger canon) and wishes there had been a woman on that list. [25:00–26:30]
- **Retention and money flows.** Dagher thinks the harder problem is keeping women in finance, with mentoring, sponsorship, childcare and flexibility all in play, and she notes how little of the money under management is run by women. Some Silicon Valley women VCs deliberately steer women founders to women investors. [26:30–29:30]
- **Not only a women's book.** Dagher says men, including a CEO, wrote to say it changed how they mentor. [30:00–32:30]

## Buffett, Munger and Graham links
- Buffett's "Mr. Market" (Graham, *The Intelligent Investor*, ch. 8) turns losses into information; resilience is the personal version.
- Munger often says to invert and learn from failures, such as the "Poor Charlie's Almanack" talk on misjudgment (1995); this episode is the career version.
- Mentorship across generations matches Buffett's account of Graham's role in his own career.

## Words to know
- **Resilience**: recovering from a setback and carrying on.
- **Pivot**: changing method while keeping your core goal.
- **Tall poppy syndrome**: the tendency to cut down people who stand out.
