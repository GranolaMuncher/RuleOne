# 152 · Sheri Salata on Radical Self-Care and Your Story

**In one sentence:** A former Oprah Winfrey Show executive producer argues that the story you tell yourself drives your habits and your money, and Phil and Danielle tie that to the story you build for a company and to the mission that made investing interesting for Danielle.

## Key ideas
- **Guest.** Sheri Salata ran the Oprah Winfrey Show for its last years and later co-led the Oprah Winfrey Network. She now co-runs a media company called Story and a podcast. [00:04–03:00]
- **Her path.** She left pre-med, worked in 7-Eleven management, and quit at 27 on the brink of a corporate promotion when she imagined herself at 60 in a glass tower. [04:00–09:00]
- **Misery as a compass.** Phil's image: holding your hand in a fire while being told it's good for you. Sheri says happiness should be the compass rather than change-or-die. [09:00–13:00]
- **Money and meaning.** Her first job at the show was entry-level at 35, and she stopped worrying about pay once her work had meaning. Danielle makes the same connection: investing became interesting when she found companies with a mission she wanted to support. [16:00–19:00]
- **85% of capital is voted by others.** Phil repeats that most invested money is managed by fund managers (he gives California teachers' pensions as an example) who vote it for returns, not for owners' values. His figure; not checked here. [19:00–22:00]
- **Beliefs about money.** Sheri sees money as an exchange and a vehicle for choice; a scarcity upbringing can block this. Phil describes his own father, shaped by the Depression and war, as unable to take financial risks. [22:00–29:00]
- **Generational wealth.** Phil uses it broadly: a life that is well in many categories. He mentions seeing Munger at an annual meeting in Pasadena with a room of people who had followed Graham, Munger and Buffett. [31:00–34:00]
- **What "story" means in Rule #1.** Phil and Danielle define it as the case for buying a company: what it is, its moat, who runs it, its price, and its mission. Then you invert it and rebut. [34:00–36:00]
- **Sheri's story.** The most important story is the one you tell yourself about your life, body, money and love. Taking authorship of it is the practical side of putting happiness first. [36:00–38:00]
- **Her practice.** Meditation twice a day, yoga, exercise, plant-based diet, daydreaming, and "touching" seven pillars each day. Her list is personal, not a method for investors. [38:00–43:00]
- **Rewrite the habit story.** Danielle struggles to keep up meditation. Sheri's fix: change the story from "I should" to "I'm someone who sits every day", and treat it as non-negotiable. She cites Gretchen Rubin's idea of a type that responds only to identity. [43:00–48:00]
- **Hope over expectation.** A story based on "I want to" works better than one based on "I have to". Danielle says her old story about investing ("scary") changed after learning to see it differently. [48:00–51:00]

## Words to know
- **Story (Rule #1 sense)**: your written case for owning a company, including its mission and price.
- **Mission**: what the company does in the world that you want to support.
- **Dharma**: Phil's word for your right path or calling.
- **Radical self-care**: Sheri's term for making your own happiness a first priority.
