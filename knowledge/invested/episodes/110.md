# 110 · From the Vault: Guy Spier on Finding a Company's Moat

**In one sentence:** A rerun of [085](085.md), Guy Spier's second interview, covering how to learn what you don't know, why managing your own money is an advantage and why moats are harder to see; the original notes are the main record.

## What's new in this airing
- Danielle's intro says the break continues and points to Berkshire's annual meeting news. Nothing else is added. [00:00–01:00]

## Key ideas (see 085 for detail)
- **Test your understanding in public.** Guy publishes short white papers (credit card networks, interest rates), asks for corrections and credits contributors. This shows how much others really know. [01:00–07:00]
- **The market doesn't always know more than you.** Borrowing a thought from Prem Watsa: don't assume others know more, except where information is withheld. [07:00–09:00]
- **Own-money advantage.** Professionals feel pressure to be seen acting (the institutional imperative). Buffett dropped an investment when his friend left the company, free of fear of being disliked. [08:00–12:00]
- **Handling emotion.** Position sizing keeps a 50% fall bearable, and strong relationships (his wife, his father) help through good and bad runs. Handle it just "less badly" than average. [12:00–19:00]
- **Value investors aren't hermits.** Buffett and Munger talked to people and visited companies. [19:00–21:00]
- **Moats.** Technology is eroding many, and obvious moats are expensively priced. Looking at many businesses over time builds the skill (the truffle-hunting image), and good businesses buy good businesses. He also says honestly that he often feels ignorant and fearful. [21:00–31:00]
