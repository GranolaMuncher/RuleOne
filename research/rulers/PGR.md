## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Progressive sells auto (and some home and commercial) insurance, priced finely by risk, direct and through agents, and invests the float.
- Revenue was $87.7B in 2025 (+16%). Nine-month 2026 channel data (secondary source): agency auto $22.8B (+5%, combined ratio 85.1), direct auto $31.2B (+11%, 89.6) [3][6]. The share of commercial auto and property was not confirmed. The hook is price (telematics, low-cost direct underwriting, heavy advertising), not lock-in. Rivals: GEICO, State Farm, Allstate, Travelers.
- Weather: Q3 2026 net premiums written $22.3B (+4%; September +2%), policies in force +6% to 40.5M, average written premium per policy about -2% (September about -3.8%) [3]. The hard-market run is over and the auto cycle is softening. Claims inflation, tariffs on parts and weather are system-wide drivers (price, not value; METHOD 7).
- **Circle of competence: 4/5.** The underwriting is easy to explain. Reserving and float investment are what I'd still need to learn.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** cost advantage (nine-month expense ratio 20.3 against 21.6 a year earlier [3]; peer ratios unconfirmed) plus data and pricing skill. *Statement:* Progressive can price risk more finely than rivals at a lower expense base, so it grows while holding margins. *Replication test:* a rival with the same market cap would need years to match the data and telematics. *Disappears test:* drivers would switch quickly, so this is cost and skill, not switching costs. *Pricing power:* limited; insurance is bought on price. A brand isn't automatically a moat (METHOD 3). ROIC: 12.7% (2015), 33.5% (2020), 4.5% (2022), 37.3% (2025). High but volatile; it collapsed in 2022 when claims inflation outran rates.
- **Management:** shares flat (587M in 2021, 588M in 2025). **Correction to my 2026-10-24 dossier:** debt is not $2.7B (that was 2015 data). The Q2 2026 10-Q shows debt of $8.39B, up from $6.90B at year-end 2025, against shareholders' equity of $34.3B (about 20% debt-to-capital) [4]. That's 0.5 years of FCF, still low, but rising. Six-month 2026 dividends paid were $8.0B (the annual variable dividend) and buybacks $1.07B [4]. Candid, rule-based reporting (a 96 combined ratio target, monthly results). Proxy pay and insider trading not read.
- **Values:** an insurer with no obvious conflict; the owner decides.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At a $136B market cap against normalised net income of ~$8B (my estimate), that's ~17x for a cyclical business. No at $231; yes at about $135 (10x).

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** Progressive has taken auto-insurance share profitably for a decade with a low-cost, data-led model. If it keeps writing at a 92-96 combined ratio while growing policies ~6%, EPS compounds at ~8% off a normalised base and the stock is a good buy near 10x normalised earnings.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) calendar-year combined ratio stays below 96 on a rolling 3-year basis; (2) policy growth stays ≥5% a year; (3) reserves stay adequate (prior-year development not turning adverse).
- **Inversion:** bear case: the cycle is turning (premium per policy falling, accident-year ratio rising, reserve releases ending), EPS drops toward $13 or lower, and the stock trades on a peak multiple. Rebuttal (partial concession): growth and the expense edge should hold share, but the price today leaves no margin of safety for it.
- **Sell triggers:** calendar-year combined ratio above 100 for two quarters; adverse prior-year development above 2 points; policy growth turning negative; ROIC below 12% for two years; debt above 30% of capital; a new CEO with a change in the 96 target.
