# PGR · Progressive Corporation

> **Verdict: WATCH** (confidence 4/5 in the business) · price $228.07 on 2026-10-23 · tranches $190 / $160 / $130 · trim above $300

But the screen's price levels treat peak-cycle EPS ($19.93) as a base to grow at 15%. Premium growth has slowed to 4%, average premium per policy is falling, and the accident-year loss ratio is rising while reserve releases hold the reported combined ratio down. ## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Progressive sells auto (and some home and commercial) insurance, priced finely by risk, direct and through agents, and invests the float.
- Revenue was $87.7B in 2025 (+16%). The split of personal auto, commercial auto and property by percent was not confirmed (10-K not read). Customers are drivers; the hook is price (telematics/Snapshot, lowest-cost direct underwriting and heavy advertising) rather than lock-in. Rivals: GEICO, State Farm, Allstate, Travelers, and Chubb in other lines.
- Weather: Q3 2026 net premiums written $22.3B (+4%) and policies in force up 6% to 40.5M, but average premium per policy fell ~2% (actuary.info) [3]. After a strong hard-market run the auto cycle is softening. Rates, tariffs on parts, used-car prices and weather are the system-wide drivers (price, not value; METHOD 7).
- **Circle of competence: 4/5.** The underwriting is easy to explain; reserving and float investment are the parts I'd still need to learn.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** cost advantage (low expense ratio ~20% against peers in the mid-20s to 30s; the exact peer numbers are unconfirmed) plus data and pricing skill. *Statement:* Progressive can price risk more finely than rivals and keep a lower expense base, so it can grow while keeping margins. *Replication test:* a rival with the same market cap would take years to build comparable data and telematics. *Disappears test:* drivers would find substitutes quickly, so the moat is cost and skill, not switching. *Pricing power:* limited; insurance is bought on price and the cycle does the work. A brand isn't automatically a moat (METHOD 3). ROIC: 12.7% (2015) → 33.5% (2020) → 4.5% (2022) → 37.3% (2025); high but volatile, and it fell in 2022 when claims inflation outran rates.
- **Management:** shares flat (587M in 2021, 588M in 2025). Debt $2.7B, 0.17 years of FCF. Candid and rule-based: a 96 combined ratio target, monthly results, a variable annual dividend. Proxy pay and insider trading not read.
- **Values:** an insurer with no obvious conflict; the owner decides.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At ~$134B market cap and normalised net income of ~$8B (my estimate, below), that's ~17x for a cyclical, so not at $228. At $160 (12x), yes.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** Progressive has been taking auto-insurance share profitably for a decade with a low-cost, data-led model. If it keeps writing at a 92-96 combined ratio while growing policies ~6%, EPS compounds at ~8% off a normalised base and the stock is a good buy at a P/E near 12-14x.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) calendar-year combined ratio stays below 96 on a rolling 3-year basis; (2) policy growth stays ≥5% a year; (3) reserves stay adequate (prior-year development not turning adverse).
- **Inversion:** bear case — the cycle is turning: average premiums fall, accident-year results worsen, release of old reserves ends, and EPS drops toward $13 or lower while the stock still trades on a peak multiple. Rebuttal (partial concession): the growth and expense advantage should hold market share, but the price today doesn't leave a margin of safety for that outcome.
- **Sell triggers:** calendar-year combined ratio above 100 for two quarters; adverse prior-year development above 2 points; policy growth turning negative; ROIC below 12% for two years; a new CEO with a change in the 96 target.
