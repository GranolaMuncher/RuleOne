## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Kinsale underwrites hard-to-place commercial insurance in the US excess & surplus (E&S) market, mostly small accounts sold through wholesale brokers, using its own technology to keep costs low.
- Money: premiums plus investment income. Q2 2026 gross written premiums were $527.6M (−5%); commercial property fell 32.7% while the rest grew 3.7% [1][2]. Net earned premium was $417.6M (+8.9%), net investment income $55.7M [2]. Division mix as a % of revenue: not found. Customers: small businesses unable to buy standard-market cover. Hook: broker relationships, speed and underwriting appetite. Rivals: RLI, W.R. Berkley, Markel, Palomar, Skyward, and standard carriers that dip into E&S when rates are good [3].
- Weather: the E&S cycle is turning soft. Lockton reports non-habitational property rates down 5–10% at renewal, and surplus capital is at records [3]. Catastrophe exposure is limited but present (Q2 cat losses were small).
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** I understand the model; I still need to learn the reserve adequacy (long-tail casualty), investment portfolio risk and the divisional mix. Reserve and business-description facts here are from general knowledge and the 10-K was not re-read [4].

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** low-cost operator (expense ratio ~21.7%) plus underwriting discipline in a niche; the "technology-enabled low cost" claim is management's [2]. Replication test: any capitalised insurer can write E&S, so the edge is the cost base, data and culture. Disappears test: brokers would notice, but would switch quickly. Pricing power: weak by nature in a competitive market; the evidence is a combined ratio of 75.5% against peers' higher ones, and ROIC 17–25% over five years. That is a durable edge only while discipline holds.
- **Management:** CEO and chairman Michael Kehoe; capital allocation is shown by a $100M buyback in Q2 and a new $250M authorisation [3], while the share count was flat at ~23.3M since 2023. Debt $225M = 0.2 years of FCF. Cash conversion 1.8 is meaningless for an insurer (float). Incentives, candour and insider trading: not read this run.
- **Values:** nothing that the owner would veto was found. Insurance for hard-to-place risks is a normal business.
- Would I buy the whole company at this price? **Not yet**: ~$7.8B for roughly $0.5B of normalised earnings (about 15x) is fair, not cheap, for a business entering a softer cycle.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** Kinsale is the lowest-cost specialist in a market that keeps growing, earning a 20%+ return on capital. A soft cycle slows premium growth for a few years but not its underwriting profit, and excess capital goes into buybacks. Buy it if the price falls to roughly 10–13x normalised earnings.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) combined ratio stays below ~85%; (2) premium growth outside property stays positive; (3) reserves hold (no adverse development).
- **Inversion:** bear case: competition cuts casualty rates too, the profitable 2020–2025 vintages were helped by hard pricing and favourable reserves (4.5 points in Q2), and the 30%+ growth of 2023–25 will not return. Rebuttal: costs are lowest in class, so margin has room to fall and stay good. Concession: the 75% combined ratio is not a normal level; earnings of ~$22/share (operating, my estimate) may be a peak.
- **Sell triggers:** a combined ratio above 90% for two quarters; adverse reserve development in consecutive years; ROIC below 12% for two years; a new CEO plus guidance cut; debt above 3x FCF.
