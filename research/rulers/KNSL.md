## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Kinsale underwrites hard-to-place commercial insurance in the US excess & surplus (E&S) market, mostly small accounts sold through wholesale brokers, using its own technology to keep costs low.
- Money: premiums plus investment income. Q3 2026 gross written premiums were $462.9M (−4.8%): property divisions −19.3% (commercial property −26.8%), casualty +0.9% [1]. Net earned premium was $423.5M (+3.0%) and net investment income $58.3M (+17.5%) [1]. Division mix as a % of revenue: not found. Customers: small businesses unable to buy standard-market cover. Hook: broker relationships, speed and appetite. Rivals: RLI, W.R. Berkley, Markel, Palomar, Skyward, and standard carriers that move into E&S when rates are good [4].
- Weather: the E&S cycle is turning soft. Lockton reports non-habitational property rates down 5–10% at renewal and surplus capital at records [4]. Catastrophe exposure is limited but present.
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** I understand the model. Still to learn: reserve adequacy on long-tail casualty, the investment portfolio (Q3 included a $37.6M pre-tax "repositioning" hit [1]) and the division mix. I did not re-read the 10-K this run [5].

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** low-cost operator (expense ratio 22.0%, up from 21.0%) plus underwriting discipline in a niche; "technology-enabled low cost" is management's claim [1]. Replication test: any capitalised insurer can write E&S, so the edge is the cost base, data and culture. Disappears test: brokers would notice but could switch. Pricing power: weak by nature in a competitive market; the evidence is a combined ratio of 75.4% against 74.9% a year ago and ROIC of 17–25% over five years. A durable edge only while discipline holds.
- **Management:** CEO and chairman Michael Kehoe. Buybacks: $100.0M in Q3 at an average of $371.74 (269,004 shares), after $100M in Q2; $237.5M of authorisation remains [1]. That price is about 4.4x book and close to today's price, so it is fair rather than cheap (METHOD 4). Share count flat at ~23.3M since 2023; dividend $0.25 a quarter. Debt $225M = 0.2 years of FCF. Cash conversion 1.89 is meaningless for an insurer (float). Book value per share was $85.25 at 30 September against $84.66 at year-end 2025 [1], so book has barely grown this year after buybacks and the repositioning. **CFO:** Bryan Petrucelli moves to the board on 22 October and retires from the CFO role on 2 March 2027; Christopher Tangard, chief accounting officer since March 2024 and at the company since 2019 (ex-KPMG audit), takes over [2]. An orderly internal succession. Incentives, candour and insider trading: not read this run.
- **Values:** nothing that the owner would veto was found.
- Would I buy the whole company at this price? **Not yet**: ~$8.3B for roughly $0.52B of operating earnings (about 16x) and 4.3x book is a fair-to-full price for a business entering a softer cycle.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** Kinsale is the lowest-cost specialist in a growing market, earning a 20%+ return on capital. A soft cycle slows premium growth for a few years but not its underwriting profit, and excess capital goes into buybacks. It becomes a buy at roughly 8–10x operating earnings.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) combined ratio stays below ~85%; (2) casualty premium growth stays positive while property shrinks; (3) reserves hold (no adverse development).
- **Inversion:** bear case: competition cuts casualty rates too, the 2020–2025 vintages were helped by hard pricing and favourable reserves (4.5 points in Q3), and the 30%+ growth of 2023–25 will not return. Rebuttal: costs are lowest in class, so margin has room to fall and stay good. Concession: a 75% combined ratio is not normal; operating EPS of ~$22.80 may be a peak, and book value has hardly grown in 2026.
- **Sell triggers:** a combined ratio above 90% for two quarters; adverse reserve development in consecutive years; ROIC below 12% for two years; a new CEO plus guidance cut; debt above 3x FCF.
