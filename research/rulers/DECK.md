## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Deckers designs and sells footwear brands, mainly HOKA running shoes and UGG boots, through wholesale partners and its own stores and websites.
- Q2 FY27: revenue $1.481B, HOKA $677M (46%), UGG $786M (53%); "other brands" are being wound down (guided -50%). Direct-to-consumer fell 1.8%, wholesale rose 5.5% [1]. Customers are runners and fashion/comfort buyers. Hooks: brand and product design, not switching costs. Rivals: On, Nike, Adidas, Brooks, New Balance, Skechers, and in boots Australian-style copies and Crocs.
- Weather: US tariffs (the refunds are one-time; the ongoing tariff cost isn't quantified here), consumer spending, and fashion cycles: UGG's history includes a 2016-17 collapse (FY17 net income $5.7M). Sourcing is concentrated in Asia (Vietnam, China; shares unconfirmed).
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** I understand the products and channels. I'd need to learn HOKA's repurchase rates, UGG's non-winter share, wholesale inventory levels, and the tariff exposure.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** brand, with some design secret (HOKA's cushioning). *Statement:* runners and style buyers pay a premium for the name. *Replication test:* a rival could not copy the brand quickly, but On, Nike and others copy the product category. *Disappears test:* HOKA fans would notice; fashion buyers would switch. *Pricing power:* gross margin was stable at HOKA/UGG scale but fails the marker (std 3.5 points; it moved with discounting and tariffs). A brand isn't automatically a moat (METHOD 3, ep 460); this is closer to a product-cycle business (METHOD 3, "tech split" logic), so the 10-year ROIC is evidence the moat existed, not that it will last. ROIC: 13% (FY16), -0.2% (FY17), 15% (FY18), 25-35% since, 40% in FY26.
- **Management:** debt $0; net cash $1.6B; the share count fell from 198M (FY16) to 146M (FY26), a 3% a year drop. Buyback prices against value were not checked. Cash conversion 110%. CEO Stefano Caroti is quoted on expense control [1]. The Item 5.02 8-K was not read; proxy pay and insider trading not found.
- **Values:** nothing flagged (not checked: supply-chain labour practices, animal-sourced sheepskin for UGG). The owner decides.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At ~$11B market cap, $1.4B owner earnings and $1.6B cash, yes if growth is ~6% and HOKA/UGG hold; the downside is a fashion fade. Leaning yes below ~$75.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** HOKA and UGG are two global brands with a debt-free, buyback-heavy business that converts earnings to cash at over 100%. If HOKA keeps taking share in running shoes and UGG holds its post-2017 comeback, 6-8% growth on a 12-16x P/E gives a fair value of ~$60-75; the price today is above it.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) HOKA keeps growing at least mid-to-high single digits with gross margin ≥55%; (2) UGG stays out of the 2016-17 pattern (revenue not falling two years running); (3) net cash stays positive.
- **Inversion:** bear case — both brands are in their late-cycle: DTC is falling, wholesale is channel-stuffing, tariffs eat margin and growth is near 0 within two years, so EPS falls and the P/E drops to 10x. Rebuttal (partial concession): the balance sheet makes it survivable, but the Sticker is already low and the price may need to fall further.
- **Sell triggers:** HOKA or UGG revenue falling year over year for two quarters; gross margin below 50%; ROIC below 20% for two years; net debt above 1× FCF; a new CEO plus a guidance cut (the CEO change happened and is unread; check the 8-K).
