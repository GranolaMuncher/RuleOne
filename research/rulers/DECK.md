## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Deckers designs and sells footwear brands, mainly HOKA running shoes and UGG boots, through wholesale partners and its own stores and websites.
- Q2 FY27: revenue $1.481B (+3.5%), HOKA $677M (46%, +6.8%), UGG $786M (53%, +3.5%); the "other brands" are being wound down. Direct-to-consumer fell 1.8% (it rose 13% in Q1) while wholesale rose 5.5% [1]. HOKA grew 15.9% and UGG 8.2% in FY26, so both have roughly halved their growth rates. Customers are runners and fashion/comfort buyers. The hook is brand and design, not switching costs. Rivals: On, Nike, Adidas, Brooks, New Balance, Skechers, and in boots Crocs and cheaper copies.
- Weather: US tariffs (the refunds are one-time; the ongoing cost isn't quantified here), consumer spending, fashion cycles. UGG fell in 2016-17 (FY17 net income $5.7M). Sourcing is concentrated in Asia (shares unconfirmed).
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** I understand the products and channels. I'd still need HOKA repurchase rates, UGG's non-winter share, wholesale inventory levels and the tariff exposure.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** brand, with some design secret (HOKA cushioning). *Statement:* runners and style buyers pay a premium for the name. *Replication test:* the brand can't be copied quickly, but On, Nike and others copy the category. *Disappears test:* HOKA fans would notice; fashion buyers would switch. *Pricing power:* the margin marker fails (std 3.5 points; margins moved with discounts, mix and tariffs). A brand isn't automatically a moat (METHOD 3, ep 460). This is closer to a product-cycle business (METHOD 3 "tech split"), so the ROIC history shows the moat existed, not that it will last. ROIC: 13% (FY16), -0.2% (FY17), 15% (FY18), 25-37% since, 40% in FY26.
- **Management:** debt $0; shares fell from 198M (FY16) to 146M (FY26), about 3% a year; $338M of buybacks in Q1 FY27 [1]. Buyback prices against value not checked. Cash conversion 100% (screen) to 110%. CEO Stefano Caroti (since August 2024, previously chief commercial officer [4]) is quoted on expense control. The proxy (pay) was not read; insider trading not found. CFO Steve Fasching has served since 2018 (per the 2025 proxy listing; no departure found).
- **Values:** nothing flagged (not checked: supply-chain labour practices, UGG sheepskin sourcing). The owner decides.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At an $11.2B market cap, $1.4B owner earnings and ~$1.2B net cash, yes if growth is ~6% and HOKA/UGG hold; the downside is a fashion fade. Leaning yes below ~$73.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** HOKA and UGG are two global brands inside a debt-free, buyback-heavy business that converts earnings to cash at ~100%. If HOKA keeps taking share and UGG holds its post-2017 comeback, 6-8% growth on a 12-16x P/E is worth ~$38-60; the price today is above that, and the zero-growth floor is ~$73.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) HOKA keeps growing at least mid-to-high single digits with gross margin ≥55%; (2) UGG stays out of the 2016-17 pattern (revenue not falling two years running); (3) net cash stays positive.
- **Inversion:** bear case: both brands are late-cycle, DTC is falling, wholesale is channel-stuffing, tariffs eat margin and growth is near 0 within two years, so EPS falls and the P/E drops to 10x. Rebuttal (partial concession): the balance sheet makes it survivable, but the Sticker is already low and the price may need to fall further.
- **Sell triggers:** HOKA or UGG revenue falling year over year for two quarters; gross margin below 50%; ROIC below 20% for two years; net debt above 1× FCF; a new CEO plus a guidance cut (guidance cut: yes; CEO change: not found).
