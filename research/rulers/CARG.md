# CARG · CarGurus, Inc.

> **Verdict: WATCH** (confidence 3/5) · price $32.18 on 2026-10-30 · tranches $34 / $27 / $23 · trim above $55
> CarGurus sells subscriptions and advertising to car dealers on its marketplace. It has no debt, $122M of net cash, free cash flow of $315M, and shrinks its share count. The screen's $54.60 Sticker uses 15% growth at a 30x P/E; revenue is guided up 10-13% and the Street has EPS +18% and +15%, so a 12% case gives ~$33. Tranche 1 ($34) is already triggered, but the next earnings report (about 5 November, unconfirmed), the real size of stock pay, and a moat that is a brand and traffic rather than lock-in, argue for waiting for the print. ## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** CarGurus runs an online car-shopping marketplace in the US where dealers pay subscription fees (and automakers pay for advertising) to reach buyers.
- Revenue: 2025 $907.0M from continuing operations (+14%), Q4 $241.1M (+15%); Q2 2026 $251.0M (+7.2%), Q3 guide $253.5-258.5M; 2026 guide +10-13% [1][2][3]. Sources are dealer subscriptions, brand advertising and financing partnerships. Segment split is now a single segment after the CarOffer wind-down; the exact split was not found. The hook is consumer traffic (price-rating tools such as "Instant Market Value" are the product, my recollection). Rivals named in the 10-K: Autotrader, CARFAX, Cars.com, TrueCar, Edmunds, KBB, Carvana, plus Craigslist, Amazon and social marketplaces [2].
- Weather: used-car prices and dealer profitability, interest rates, and dealer budgets. Management said Q3 would see a "more measured pace" of dealer decisions [3]. Also the risk that big platforms and AI search bypass listing sites. Ownership: 81.0M Class A and 14.2M Class B shares, so the founder (executive chair Langley Steinert) keeps control through the B shares (voting split not confirmed).
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** Easy to explain, and I can use the product. I'd still need dealer counts, revenue per dealer and churn, which I did not find.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** a modest network effect: consumer traffic draws dealers, and dealer inventory draws consumers. *Statement:* CarGurus is a top-two or three independent shopping site that dealers must list on. *Replication test:* a rival with the same market cap could not easily rebuild the traffic, but Autotrader and Cars.com already hold the same audience. *Disappears test:* dealers would feel it, but they list on several sites (multi-homing), so switching costs are low. *Pricing power:* revenue per dealer has risen, but I have not seen the figure, so unconfirmed. "A brand isn't automatically a moat" (METHOD 3). ROIC (screen) was 10-22% for most of the last decade and 50.8% in 2025, but that last figure is inflated: book value per share fell from $5.40 to $3.73 as shares were bought back, so the base is shrinking. I treat the high ROIC as partly arithmetic.
- **Management:** shares fell from 128.2M (2022) to 100.4M (2025) and 95.2M in February 2026, without dilution (`no_dilution` -2.5% a year). The Board closed CarOffer, the wholesale business that had $1.66B of revenue in 2022 and was losing money, on 6 August 2025 and treated it as discontinued from 31 December 2025, an honest admission that a past deal failed [2]. Debt: none; cash conversion 179% on the screen. **SBC:** the 10-K cash-flow figure was beyond what I could read; the flag says 16% of OCF. If OCF is about $330M, SBC is about $50M a year, and that cost belongs in owner earnings. Proxy pay, insider trading (a director sold 10,000 shares; small) and call candour: not read.
- **Values:** nothing flagged. Dual-class control is a governance point the owner may weigh.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At $3.0B, about $2.9B net of cash, for roughly $260-310M of cash after stock pay, about 10-11x, yes, if the traffic holds up; I'd rather pay 8x.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** A debt-free marketplace with ~$900M of revenue that grows 10-13%, converts about a third of revenue to free cash flow, and retires ~3% of its shares a year; at ~11x cash after stock pay it pays you to wait for traffic and dealer spending to grow.
- **Three things that must stay true:**
  1. Marketplace revenue keeps growing at 10%+ with free cash flow above 25% of revenue.
  2. Dealer count and revenue per dealer do not fall.
  3. Net cash stays positive and stock pay does not exceed ~7% of revenue.
- **Inversion:** the bear case is that dealers multi-home and cut spend, Autotrader/Cox and Cars.com compete on price, AI search and automaker-direct sales reduce the role of listing sites, and the 2025 earnings jump was partly a CarOffer-exit clean-up. *Rebuttal/concession:* partly conceded; the traffic moat is shallow, and I cannot rebut dealer churn without the data. That is the main reason this is WATCH and not BUY.
- **Sell triggers:** revenue growth below 5% for two quarters; free cash flow margin below 20%; dealer count falling for three quarters; net cash turning to debt for an acquisition; or a new CEO plus a guidance cut.
