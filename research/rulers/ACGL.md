# ACGL · Arch Capital Group Ltd.

> **Verdict: WATCH** (confidence 4/5 in the business) · price $106.36 on 2026-10-30 · tranches $70 / $58 / $49 · trim above $130
> Arch is a wonderful, disciplined underwriter: no dilution, ROE of 16-18%, book value per share up from $48 (2023) to $67.90. But the BUY* on the screen rests on 15% windage applied to $12.78 of TTM EPS while Street EPS growth is +3.9% this year and -3.3% next, the combined ratio (82.5) is far better than the long-run norm, premiums are shrinking (-3% in Q3) and the stock is 0.7% below its 52-week high. At $106 it is 1.57x book and about 10x normalised earnings: fair, not cheap. Wait for a price near $70 or a real event. Not investment advice.

## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Arch writes specialty insurance, property-casualty reinsurance and mortgage insurance, and invests the float.
- Q3 2026 net premiums written $3,845M (-3.0%): insurance $2,006M (+2.7%), reinsurance $1,576M (-9.3%), mortgage $263M (-4.0%) [1]. That is about 52% / 41% / 7% of premiums written. Customers are corporate insureds and brokers (insurance), insurers seeking capacity (reinsurance) and mortgage lenders (US mortgage insurance, plus other geographies; not confirmed). Hook: underwriting reputation and capacity, not lock-in. Rivals: Chubb, Markel, W. R. Berkley, RenaissanceRe, Everest, AIG and in mortgage insurance MGIC, Radian and Essent (my list; not checked against the 10-K).
- Weather: property-casualty rates are softening after the hard market, reinsurance is the first to give (-9% premiums); catastrophes are lumpy (Q3 cats $234M, partly Iran-conflict losses in insurance) [1]. Bermuda base: a tax and regulation matter (Bermuda corporate tax rules from 2025; not researched here). Mortgage insurance depends on US housing credit; Q3 cure rates were better than expected.
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** I can explain the model. I could not judge reserve adequacy, so I would need to learn the reserve triangles and the 10-K reserve section.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** a narrow one: underwriting skill and cycle discipline (writing less when prices are poor), a cost advantage in mortgage insurance's scale, and a high bar for new capital in specialty lines. *Statement:* Arch earns above-average returns by shrinking when prices fall and growing when they rise. *Replication test:* a rival with the same market cap can raise the capital in a year but not the underwriting culture. *Disappears test:* insureds would swap to Chubb, Markel and others with little pain. *Pricing power:* none by itself; the cycle sets prices. Insurance is not a pricing-power moat, so the markers matter less than discipline. ROIC (screen): 8.7% in 2015, 6.7% in 2017, 14.2% in 2019, 24.2% in 2023, 20.7% in 2024, 18.2% in 2025; rising, but note 2023 included a one-time tax benefit (Bermuda deferred tax; unconfirmed).
- **Management:** shares fell 376M to 341M diluted in a year (Q3 diluted 341.4M against 376.1M), with $1.4B of buybacks in Q3 alone [1]. That is good only below value (METHOD 4); at ~1.57x book and ~10x normalised earnings, I would call it fair, not cheap. Debt: **the screen shows 0, which is wrong.** The 10-Q shows senior notes of $4,286M at 30 June 2026 (up from $2,729M), after a $2.0B issue in June ($600M 5.25% due 2036, $1.4B 5.95% due 2056) that repaid a $500M note and funded tenders of 2043/2046 notes [2][3]. Debt to capital rose from 10.1% to 15.1%. Interest expense rose to $62M a quarter from $37M. Against $6.06B of FCF that is 0.7 years, which still passes `low_debt` (it was 0 on the screen). Proxy pay, insider trades and letter candour: not read.
- **Values:** an insurer in Bermuda; nothing I could flag. The owner decides.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At $37.7B for about $3.5-3.7B of normalised earnings (10-11x) and 1.57x book, I would at roughly $70, not at $106.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** Arch is a cycle-disciplined underwriter that compounds book value at about 15% by underwriting well and buying back stock; at around $70 the market would be paying roughly 6.7x normalised earnings and 1.0x book for that.
- **Three things that must stay true:**
  1. Combined ratio stays below ~92 through the cycle (it is 82.5 now) with no reserve shortfall.
  2. Operating ROE stays above 12%.
  3. Buybacks happen below ~1.5x book, and debt to capital stays below ~20%.
- **Inversion:** the bear case is that the market is soft, 2023-25 earnings were peak earnings, reserves for 2019-23 accident years develop adversely, and the mortgage insurer's low loss ratio reverts (the mortgage combined ratio was 25.5, of which 15 points were favourable reserve release). *Rebuttal/concession:* partly conceded; that is why I use $10.5 and not $12.78, and a wider mortgage loss ratio would take the normalised figure lower (to ~$9.5-10).
- **Sell triggers:** combined ratio above 95 for four quarters; operating ROE below 10% for two years; debt to capital above 25%; a reserve charge of more than 3% of reserves; or a new CEO plus a change in underwriting discipline.
