## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Axos is a nationwide online bank (no branches) that gathers deposits digitally and lends against multifamily, commercial real estate, single-family mortgages and commercial & industrial loans, with a securities-clearing and advisory-custody business next to it.
- Q1 FY2027 (to 30 Sep 2026): net loans $25.9B, deposits $25.4B, total assets about $30B at June. Pretax income $175.2M: banking $176.1M, securities $10.3M, corporate -$11.2M [1]. Percent of revenue by loan type: not found (the 10-K [2] has the loan table; I did not confirm the shares). Customers are consumers (online checking and savings, Jenius Bank deposits) and commercial borrowers; the hook is price and rate on deposits and the speed of its lending. Rivals: other digital banks (Ally, Marcus, SoFi), regional banks, and Schwab/First Republic-type custody banks (my list).
- Weather: rates (NIM 4.64% against 4.75% a year ago), US commercial real estate and C&I credit, and deposit competition. Acquisitions this year: Verdant (C&I lender) and about $2.3B of Jenius Bank deposits (closed in the June 2026 quarter); the Capital One IRA deposit portfolio was announced (closing amount not found) [2][3].
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** The deposit-and-lend model is simple. I would still need to read the loan-by-loan credit tables, the C&I underwriting, and the regulatory history before putting real money in.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** a cost advantage, not a brand. No branches gives an efficiency ratio of 49.5% (48.3% a year earlier) and a margin of 4.6% against typical bank NIMs of ~3% (my recollection; not verified). *Statement:* Axos funds and lends more cheaply than a branch bank. *Replication test:* a digital bank with the same market cap can copy the model, which Ally and SoFi are doing. *Disappears test:* its depositors and borrowers would find alternatives easily. *Pricing power:* unproven; a loan book earns what credit and rates allow. A thin moat, and the sort of lender that only a credit record tests. ROIC (screen): 17.5% (2016), 12.5% (2019), 11.5% (2022), 17.2% (2024), 14.0% (2026), so at or above 10% every year, with no clear fall. For banks, ROE (15.7%) and BVPS growth (about 18% a year over 5 years) are the better measures (METHOD 5; MARKERS banks note).
- **Management:** shares 63.6M (2016) → 56.7M (2026), a fall of about 11%, with $14M of buyback at an average of $87.40 in the quarter, and $112M left [1]. CEO Greg Garrabrants speaks of a "solid" pipeline and said a few large loans did not close. Candour: the release explains the charge-off spike. Debt: the screen shows $511.6M of debt (1.2 years of FCF), but a bank's deposits are its funding, so that figure only says little. Cash conversion 135%; as for all banks, not meaningful. Incentives, insider trading and the regulatory/legal history: **not found / not read** (the company has a history of disputes and a whistleblower case in earlier years; I did not check how it ended, so I do not rely on it).
- **Values:** nothing to flag from the facts I have. The owner decides.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At $5.1B (about 1.56x book, 10.3x trailing earnings), maybe at $72 (about 8x), not at $90 until I understand the commercial loan book.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** A cost-efficient digital bank compounding book value at about 17% with ROE of 15-16%; at ~0.8-1.2x book the market would be paying little for the franchise.
- **Three things that must stay true:**
  1. Net charge-offs on loans return to well below 0.5% a year after the two C&I items.
  2. Efficiency ratio stays under ~55% and NIM above 4%.
  3. ROE stays at or above 13% with CET1 above 11%.
- **Inversion:** the bear case is that fast loan growth (loans +$4.5B in fiscal 2026) in C&I cash-flow lending and acquired books ends in credit losses, that deposits rely on rate-sensitive customers, and that efficiency is flattered by growth. *Rebuttal/concession:* partly conceded; non-performing assets are falling and capital is strong, but the evidence is one quarter and I haven't read the loan tables.
- **Sell triggers:** net charge-offs above 1% for two consecutive years; non-performing assets above 1.5% of assets; ROE below 11% for two years; CET1 below 10%; or a regulatory action against the bank.
