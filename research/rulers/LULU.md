## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** lululemon designs and sells premium athletic apparel and footwear, mostly through its own stores and website.
- Money: Q2 FY26 revenue $2.42B (−4%): Americas −8%, International +4% [1]. 825 company stores. China Mainland was growing strongly earlier in the year (+30% in Q1) but slowed to −2% comps in Q2 [1][6]. Segment shares of revenue: not found. Customer: affluent, fitness-minded women, increasingly men. Hook: brand, fit and community. Rivals: Nike, Alo Yoga, Vuori, Gymshark, On, Athleta.
- Weather: consumer discretionary; tariffs (US IEEPA tariffs were struck down and refunds began; a customer lawsuit alleges price rises during the tariff period, unproven [3]); competition in the US from fast-growing private rivals Alo and Vuori [6].
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** Understandable, and I know the product. Still to learn: why newness failed, markdown and inventory health, store productivity and the true tariff cost (a $380M figure in one outlet is unconfirmed).

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** brand, with a thin fabric-and-fit secret and a community effect. Statement: customers pay a premium for the fit and fabric identity. InvestED's view: in 2017 Phil called the moat thin ("cute", not durable) and the fabric a secret that rivals could not easily copy, while doubting management [105]; in 2021 he said the fabric "is hard to copy" and became a brand [432]. Replication test: Alo and Vuori show that a rival can build a close product, so the moat is mostly the brand. Disappears test: loyal customers would notice. Pricing power: shown by 2021–23 margins, but Q2 gross margin was 60.5% reported (+200 bps) only because the tariff refund added 560 bps, so underlying was ~54.9%, about 360 bps below last year's ~58.5% (derived), and Q1 gross margin fell 410 bps to 54.2% [6]. ROIC fell from 40.8% to 31.4%. A brand isn't automatically a moat (METHOD 3, episode 460). What has changed since 2017: free cash flow is now $0.9–1.6B, not $250M, and ROIC of 31–41% shows the moat existed. What has not: the show's doubts about fashion brands holding their appeal look closer to right than they did.
- **Management:** buybacks were heavy: 2.7M shares for $330M in Q2 (about $122 each) [1], above today's price; shares 134M (2019) to 119M (2026). Zero debt, $1.39B cash. FCF fell from $1.58B to $0.92B in the last fiscal year. Leadership: the founder fight ended in a cooperation agreement (26 May 2026) adding two of Wilson's nominees to the board and a declassified board (June) [3][6]; the previous CEO left in December 2025; Heidi O'Neill (ex-Nike) started on 8 September [1]. On 7 October she added a Chief Product Officer (Athleta CEO Maggie Gauger) and a COO (Walmart Canada's Joseph Godsey), while the supply-chain and brand leaders left [2]. Episode 210 calls a new CEO an automatic red flag and a prompt to dig deep, not an automatic no; I have started, but have no record of her capital allocation yet. Wilson's holding: the earlier dossier said ~4.3% [3]; a 2026 summary says ~8.6%. Unconfirmed; check the 13D/A. Proxy pay, insider buying: not found.
- **Values:** consumer apparel supply chains (labour practices): not researched. The owner decides.
- Would I buy the whole company at this price? **Maybe**: ~$11.3B market cap less $1.4B cash is ~$9.9B for underlying earnings of ~$1.0B. I would want proof that the US decline stops.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** a brand with 30%+ ROIC and a clean balance sheet has hit a product and execution slump. A new CEO with a product-first reset can bring sales back to flat-to-modest growth, and at ~10x underlying earnings that outcome is a good return.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) net cash is kept (no debt to fund buybacks); (2) gross margin ex one-offs stays above ~54%; (3) US comps stop declining within ~4 quarters.
- **Inversion:** bear case: the premium-athleisure fad is over, rivals (Alo, Vuori, On) took the customers, tariffs and discounting erode margin, and a new team means more resets. Rebuttal: international is still growing and ROIC is above 30%. Concession: nothing yet shows the US decline is ending, and the buybacks near $122 destroyed value.
- **Sell triggers:** ROIC below 15% for two years; gross margin below 50%; comps negative for four more quarters after the new product slate; debt issued to fund buybacks; further CEO turnover.
