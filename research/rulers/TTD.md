## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** The Trade Desk runs the software that lets advertisers and their agencies buy digital ads (mainly connected TV, mobile and display) across the open internet, charging a percentage of the money that passes through.
- How it makes money: a take rate on gross ad spend through its platform (the Kokai interface). Segment shares of revenue: not reported. By channel, industry estimates put the mix at roughly 50% CTV, 25–30% mobile, 11–15% display and 6% audio (third-party, unconfirmed) [1]. Customers: ad agencies and holding companies; two holding companies each exceeded 10% of gross billings in 2025 [3]. Client retention has exceeded 95% for over a decade [3] and was above 95% in Q2 [2]. Hook: it owns no media, so buyers can compare prices and results. Rivals: Amazon DSP (the main one), Google's DV360 (also a key inventory supplier [3]), Microsoft's Xandr, and the walled gardens of Meta and Google.
- Weather: ad budgets are cyclical, and consumer packaged goods and autos (about a quarter of platform spend) are cutting [2]. Amazon is undercutting fees and matching spend [1]. Netflix's ad sales also tie into Amazon [4]. Agency buying groups fought over OpenPath, a direct-to-publisher route: two big groups left in February 2026 and the dispute was settled on 12 June [2]. One job-board page claimed Publicis dropped TTD in March 2026; that source is weak and the claim is unconfirmed.
- **Circle of competence: 2/5.** I understand the model. I cannot yet judge how much of the share loss is price (Amazon's fees), product (Kokai) or agency politics, nor the true take-rate trend. Still to learn: gross spend growth, take rate and what Amazon wins on.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** a mix of switching cost (data, workflow, agency integration) and a neutrality brand. Statement: buyers who distrust Google, Amazon and Meta use TTD because it has no inventory to favour. Replication test: Amazon already runs a rival DSP with its own inventory and says it will match spend, so a rival with a large market cap *can* build it. Disappears test: agencies would feel it, but Amazon would take the spend. Pricing power: the evidence points the wrong way. TTD cut fees under Amazon's pressure [1], operating margin has swung widely (marker below), and revenue growth fell from 19% (Q4 2025) to 12% (Q1) to 3% (Q2), with Q3 guided to about −12% [2][1]. ROIC was 17–23% in 2016–19, 3–8% in 2021–23 and 16% in 2025 (screen).
- **Management:** founder-CEO Jeff Green. Class B shares carry ten votes; he held 97.6% of Class B and about 48% of the votes as of June 2025 [3][5]. The Class B sunset was proposed for December 2035; I did not confirm the vote outcome [5]. Capital allocation is the weak spot (METHOD 4: buy back only below value): buybacks were $1.38B in 2025, and Q4 purchases averaged $38.71 to $51.56 a share [3], against $13 now. Stock-based compensation was $491M, $495M and $492M in 2025, 2024 and 2023 [3], so the buybacks mostly offset dilution (shares 501.9M in 2024, 493.6M in 2025, about 476M in January 2026 per the 10-K cover [3]). Debt: none; $1.12B cash plus $0.36B short-term investments and an undrawn $750M facility [2]. In September it cut ~15% of staff (575–585 people), the first double-digit cut in its history; Green said the company is not distressed [6]. Cash restructuring charges are guided at $39–51M [6]. A further $350M buyback authorisation was added in February 2026 [3]; I found no purchases at today's price. Proxy pay design, candour of the calls, insider trading: not read this run.
- **Values:** advertising technology and data use. Nothing found for the owner to veto; the owner decides.
- Would I buy the whole company at this price? **Not yet.** About $6.15B for a business that earned about $370M after stock pay last year, with revenue now shrinking, does not cover the risk that the Amazon problem is permanent.

## E · Event
- What happened: Q2 revenue (reported 6 August) was $715M, +3%, against guidance of about $750M, and the shares fell 24% to $13.39 that day [2]. Q3 guidance is "at least $650M", about a 12% fall and the first yearly decline in the company's history [2]. A ~15% layoff followed on 3 September [6]. The stock is down about 68% this year and 76% from its 52-week high. Reports tie the slowdown to Amazon DSP's cut-rate competition, CPG and auto pullbacks and the OpenPath disputes [1][2]. Reported detail (secondary, unconfirmed): Q2 EPS was $0.34 against $0.40 expected, and Amazon has discounted DSP fees to as low as ~1% for big spenders against 12–15% at TTD [8]. Small positives: Skydance's CTV pause-ad deal [7], Netflix inventory opening to Kokai buyers (July) and a Gracenote deal (27 August) (search summaries; details not read).
- Event checks (METHOD 7):
  1. Known: ✔ share loss to Amazon, weak CPG and autos, agency disputes.
  2. Easy to find: ✔ it is the headline.
  3. Needs at least a year: ✔ rebuilding spend takes years.
  4. Resolves within ~3 years: ? the ad cycle will turn; whether Amazon's share gain reverses is unknown.
  5. No new debt needed: ✔ net cash.
  6. You would own it for life: ✘ not yet; that needs proof the moat survives Amazon.
- **Event or problem?** Part cycle, part possible changed story. Falling fees, falling revenue and a layoff point to competition, not a one-off. By METHOD 7, a problem that "takes a miracle" or a changed story is not an event. I cannot rule out the cyclical case, so WATCH, with low confidence. Down is not cheap: this is the value-trap question. It is not at a record high.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** An independent buying platform with no debt and above-95% client retention is in a bad patch: ad budgets are weak, Amazon competes on price and revenue is falling. If it wins back share (Kokai, CTV, retail data) and revenue grows high single digits again, earnings after stock pay of $400–500M would justify well over $13. At $8.50 or less the price assumes only that cash flow after stock pay does not fall further.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) client retention stays above 95% and gross spend keeps growing even while the take rate falls; (2) revenue returns to growth by the end of 2027; (3) management stops buying back stock above value and cuts stock pay as a share of revenue.
- **Inversion:** bear case: Amazon bundles its DSP with its own inventory, data and checkout and matches fees, so TTD loses volume and take rate for years; agencies build their own buying and push for direct deals. The layoff and the first revenue fall mean growth is over, and $490M a year of stock pay makes a low multiple of "cash flow" a trap. Rebuttal: retention is above 95%, there is no debt and the street's EPS for next year is only −13%. Concession: I cannot rebut Amazon with the evidence in hand. I did not find Amazon's share of CTV spend or TTD's take rate, so the rebuttal is incomplete.
- **Sell triggers (if owned):** revenue down year on year for a second straight year; client retention below 90%; take rate falling for four quarters in a row; buybacks above value with the share count still not falling; ROIC below 8% for two years; Green gives up control or leaves alongside a guidance cut.
