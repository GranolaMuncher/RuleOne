# FUTU · Futu Holdings Ltd

> **Verdict: WATCH** (confidence 2/5) · price $99.19 on 2026-10-23 · tranches $85 / $75 / $62 · trim above $150
> Futu (moomoo) is a high-return, debt-free broker whose revenue grew 36% and net income 42% in Q2 2026 [1]. The shares fell about 51% after China's securities regulator (CSRC) proposed a RMB 1.85B (~$271M) penalty and a two-year run-off of mainland clients [2][4]. The penalty is about 2% of the market cap and mainland clients are ~13% of funded accounts, so this could be a real event. But the penalty is not confirmed final, Chinese political risk has decided other calls (Alibaba), and the screen's cash-flow prices are not usable for a broker. At $99 there is no margin of safety against my own Sticker (about $149, MOS about $75). Research, not advice.

## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Futu runs the moomoo and Futu NiuNiu apps, an online broker that lets retail investors (mostly in Hong Kong, Singapore, the US, Australia and elsewhere, and until now mainland China) trade US, Hong Kong and China A-shares, and earns commissions, interest on client margin and cash, and wealth-management fees.
- Money: Q2 2026 revenue HK$7.20B (US$918M), +35.6%; net income HK$3.64B (US$464M), +41.6%; funded accounts 3.84M (+33.6%); client assets HK$1.40 trillion (+43.6%); trading volume +78.8% [1]. Revenue split by commission / interest / other: not found this run. Rivals: Interactive Brokers, Robinhood, Tiger (UP Fintech), Webull, local banks and brokers. Mainland China funded accounts were ~13% of the total at the end of Q1 2026 [2].
- Weather: retail trading activity (cyclical: volume +79% is a hot market), interest rates (interest income), and the China regulator. The CSRC first told Futu and Tiger to stop taking new mainland clients in 2022, and in 2026 proposed the penalty plus a two-year wind-down in which existing mainland clients can only sell or withdraw [2][3][4]. Class actions followed, which are allegations [5].
- **Circle of competence: 2/5.** I understand a broker's economics. I do not understand the CSRC's intent, how clients are retained abroad, or how client money and margin lending are accounted for. To learn: the 20-F on client-money and margin-loan accounting, the revenue split, the share of new accounts from mainland-born customers living abroad.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** a brand and product in cross-border investing (US stock access from Asia), low-cost scale, and some switching cost in the account and assets. Replication test: IBKR, Robinhood and Webull compete for the same customers, and brokerage fees fall in competition. Disappears test: customers would switch, at some cost. Pricing power: not shown; commission rates are competitive. ROIC (`history_usd`): 13–29% in 2020–25, rising to 29% in 2025; it is a broker's ROIC on a small equity base, so I read it as ROE (~28% in 2025: net income $1.45B on book value ~$5.1B).
- **Management:** founder-CEO Li Hua (a personal fine of RMB 1.25M proposed [2]). Debt: none on the screen; the screen does not capture broker margin funding and client liabilities (unconfirmed). Shares: 1.22B (2021) to 1.13B (2025) so buybacks have outpaced issuance since 2021, but the five-year marker (from 2020) shows +1.5% a year. Proxy and incentives: foreign private issuer, not read. Candour: the licensing breach was alleged to run from 2023 [5]; the regulator's finding is a question about compliance culture. Insider buying: none found.
- **Values:** retail trading and margin lending can encourage over-trading. The owner decides.
- Would I buy the whole company at this price? **Probably yes outside China, not yet knowingly.** Market cap $14.0B against ~$1.4B TTM earnings (10x) and a ~$1.9B run-rate is cheap for this growth and ROE. What stops me is whether the regulatory end-state is solved and whether the Q2 earnings level is cyclical peak.

## E · Event

Whether the penalty is now final and the amount booked in Q2: not found. Futu said it can submit statements and request a hearing [2]. Q2 results (20 Aug) were strong regardless [1].

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** the best-run cross-border retail broker in Asia keeps taking share in Hong Kong, Singapore, the US and Australia; the mainland run-off costs ~13% of accounts and one fine, then fades; with no debt and ~28% ROE, earnings compound at ~12%.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) the CSRC's action stops at the penalty and run-off, with no restrictions on Futu's Hong Kong licence or app availability; (2) funded accounts outside mainland keep growing by 20% or more; (3) net income stays above ~$1.2B through a market downturn (commissions plus interest).
- **Inversion:** bear case: Q2 is a peak (volume +79%, high interest income), the mainland run-off is bigger than 13% in revenue (affluent mainland users trade more), Beijing pushes further (app removal, HK licence pressure) and the ADS is delisted or discounted like Alibaba. Rebuttal: a debt-free balance sheet absorbs the fine; the street's EPS growth for next year remains +27%. Concession: I can't size mainland revenue or rule out further action.
- **Sell triggers (if owned):** a new CSRC action touching Hong Kong or non-mainland users; non-mainland funded accounts growing under 10%; net income below $1.0B in any TTM; a dilutive acquisition or a regulatory-capital call; ROE under 15% for two years.
