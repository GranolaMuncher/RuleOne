## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Intuit sells the software that small businesses and households use to do their books, payroll, payments and taxes (QuickBooks, TurboTax, Credit Karma, Mailchimp).
- Revenue was $21.4B in FY26 (July year-end), up 14%. The segment split by percent is not confirmed here (not fetched); "Big Bets" were reported as 30% of revenue, up 34% (247wallst). Customers are small businesses, consumers filing US taxes and lenders buying leads on Credit Karma. Hooks: QuickBooks holds a business's financial records, payroll and bank connections; TurboTax holds years of filing history. Rivals: H&R Block, FreeTaxUSA/Cash App Taxes and the IRS Direct File fallback, Xero, Sage, Bill.com, and now general AI assistants.
- Weather: the US tax-filing market is mature (TurboTax unit growth guided at only 2-3%). AI is cutting the cost of both filing and bookkeeping. Management said it "lost quality DIY customers to low-cost providers this year" (247wallst). Mailchimp sales were weak and desktop products are declining (per search summary).
- **Circle of competence: 3/5.** I understand the franchise. I would still need to learn: churn in QuickBooks Online after price rises, the share of TurboTax revenue from assisted/expert products, the stock-comp number, and why OCF jumped 42% in FY26.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** switching cost plus data/network (accountants and small-business records inside QuickBooks), and brand in TurboTax. *Statement:* businesses don't move their ledgers and payroll lightly. *Replication test:* a rival can copy the screens quickly now (AI), but not the bank feeds, accountant channel and years of records. *Disappears test:* small-business bookkeeping would be disrupted for a long time. *Pricing power:* revenue grew 16% a year for ten years with steady margins (margin_stable passes, std 2.7 points), but the FY27 guide shows pricing and volume are now slowing. A brand isn't automatically a moat (METHOD 3, ep 460), and the new DIY losses to low-cost rivals are a direct moat test.
- **ROIC:** 42% (FY16) → 9.1% (FY22) → 17.6% (FY26). The drop followed Credit Karma (2020) and Mailchimp (2021) deals paid for with stock and debt. It recovers, but the 3-year average is 65% of the 10-year average, so `roic_not_falling` fails. Capital allocation on Mailchimp looks poor (sales weak, per search); the price paid and any impairment are unconfirmed.
- **Management:** debt $7.7B, or 0.9 years of FCF; net debt $3.0B. Cash conversion 190% (owner earnings $10.1B against net income $4.6B), which is high because owner earnings add back stock-based comp and tax items; I have not confirmed the SBC figure, so the Ten Cap is probably generous. Shares fell from 283M to 277M in FY26 (dilution marker passes). CEO Sasan Goodarzi was candid in the call ("AI will be a disruptor, and we intend to be the disruptor"). Proxy incentives, insider trading: not found.
- **Values:** nothing flagged. Intuit lobbied against free government tax filing in past years (well known, not re-confirmed). The owner decides.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At ~$95B market cap and $10B of owner earnings, probably yes if the moat holds; at 20.9x TTM EPS it's cheap against its 49x median. Not yet, because I can't confirm the moat after the AI shock.

Shares fell about 12% to ~$316 and kept sliding (-50% off the high) even after the guide was reaffirmed at the 2026-09-17 investor day [1][2][3]. ## S · Story
- **Thesis:** Intuit is the system of record for small-business and household finances, with a 40% FCF margin and almost no debt. The market prices it as a victim of AI. If QuickBooks keeps adding customers and TurboTax holds its funnel, owner earnings of $8-10B on a $95B company is cheap. If AI lets rivals take the DIY and small-business base at lower prices, growth settles near 5-9% and today's price is fair.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) revenue growth stays ≥9% in FY27 and FY28; (2) TurboTax units stop shrinking in share (not only 2-3% growth); (3) ROIC stays above 15% with no more large stock-financed deals.
- **Inversion:** bear case — AI agents file taxes and keep books for almost nothing, so price per customer falls, Mailchimp is a write-off and the stock-comp-adjusted earnings are $23 and falling. Rebuttal (partial concession): the deepest moat is data and distribution, not software, and the FY27 guide is still growth. But the guide already admits lost customers, and I can't call it a one-time problem.
- **Sell triggers:** revenue growth below 6% for two years; TurboTax units down year over year; ROIC below 12% for two years; net debt above 3× FCF; a large acquisition paid in stock.
