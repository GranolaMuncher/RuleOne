## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** NetEase develops and operates online games (mostly PC and mobile, in China and increasingly abroad), plus a language-learning business (Youdao) and a music streaming service (Cloud Music).
- Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-20): net revenue RMB30.1B (+7.9%); games and related services RMB25.0B (+9.7%, ~83% of revenue; online games ~97.7% of that segment); Youdao RMB1.5B (+3.5%); Cloud Music RMB2.0B (flat). H1: revenue RMB60.7B (+7.0%), games RMB50.7B (+8.3%) [1]. The PC/mobile split was not disclosed in the release. Customers are players; the hooks are franchise communities that last for years, and live-service updates. Rivals: Tencent, miHoYo, Krafton, Western publishers.
- Weather: China is the main risk. Regulation (the December 2023 draft rules to curb gaming spending knocked the stock down by roughly a quarter, per one report [3]), game licensing, US-China politics (ADR delisting risk, unconfirmed this year), and the CNY. Games are hit-driven: a new title can fail, though the old franchises give a base.
- **Circle of competence: 2/5.** I understand what the games business is. I'd still need to learn the title-by-title revenue mix, how much comes from the three oldest games, the regulatory calendar, the VIE structure of the listing, and how restricted the cash is.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** a mix of community (network effects in live-service games) and brand across a portfolio of long-running games. *Statement:* NetEase has a stream of games that keep players for a decade, and the platform to operate them at high margin. *Replication test:* a rival can build a game; it can't quickly build an established player community or a 20-year operating record. *Disappears test:* players would switch to other games; the loss would hurt the owners but not the world, so the moat is weaker than a toll road. *Pricing power:* gross margin rose to ~70.5% in Q2 from ~64.7% a year earlier (my calculation from the release [1]); the earlier ten-year history fails the stability marker. ROIC: 21% (2015), 14% (2020), 19% (2025). This shows the moat existed. A product-cycle business (METHOD 3 "tech split") needs new hits, so I treat it as a moat I can't count on.
- **Management:** CEO William Ding is the founder. Capital allocation: debt ~$0.06B against net cash; H1 2026 dividends paid RMB8.3B and share repurchases RMB3.1B (ADSs and subsidiary shares) [1]; shares fell 0.8% a year over five years. Q2 included a RMB2.95B net investment loss (fair-value declines on equity securities and impairments), which tells me the company holds equity stakes in other companies (a capital-allocation distraction, and it cut Q2 net income to RMB7.0B from 8.6B). The size of those stakes is unconfirmed. Operating income rose to RMB12.1B from 9.1B. Proxy-style incentives and insider ownership: not found (as a foreign private issuer it files a 20-F, which I did not read).
- **Values:** the owner decides; I did not check content-regulation or labour issues beyond noting the China exposure.
- **Would I buy the whole company at this price?** At a $73B market cap against ~$25B of net cash and ~$8.4B owner earnings, it's about 6x owner earnings ex-cash. Yes in principle, if the cash is really accessible and the games last; I can't yet answer the China and cash questions, so the answer is "not yet".

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** NetEase turns a portfolio of durable online games into ~44% FCF margins and returns cash to owners, with a net cash pile worth a third of its market value. If revenue keeps growing 8-10% and the cash is accessible, the business is worth ~$95-105 and the stock is a buy below that with a margin of safety.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) games revenue keeps growing at least mid-single digits with gross margin ≥60%; (2) net cash stays positive and is not trapped or squandered on investments (equity-investment losses stay small against operating income); (3) no regulation that cuts player spending materially.
- **Inversion:** bear case: Beijing tightens gaming rules again, new hits fail, a few old titles carry the profit and fade, the net cash is partly in China and partly claimed by minorities, and the ADR faces US-China delisting pressure. Rebuttal (partial concession): the business has survived several regulatory cycles and has a clean balance sheet, but the political risk can't be modelled, which is why competence is 2/5 and tranches are set below the point where I'd call it cheap.
- **Sell triggers:** games revenue falling for two quarters; gross margin below 55%; ROIC below 10% for two years; a new gaming regulation that cuts monthly spending limits materially; net cash used on large acquisitions or equity stakes with losses above 25% of operating income two years running; delisting action on the ADR.
