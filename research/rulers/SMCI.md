## U · Understand
- **The business in one sentence:** Super Micro designs and assembles server systems (now mostly AI racks built around Nvidia GPUs) and sells them to data-centre operators and enterprises.
- Money: system sales, almost all hardware. FY2026 net sales $39.06B (+78%); the US was 70.9% of sales, Asia 15.5%, Europe 6.8% [3]. Growth came from "large design wins from a few customers" [2]; the customer split is not quantified in what I read, so concentration is unconfirmed but likely high. Rivals: Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Taiwanese ODMs (Foxconn, Quanta, Wiwynn). Related parties Ablecom and Compuware provide design and manufacturing coordination [2].
- Weather: demand for AI capacity (a cycle), Nvidia allocation and pricing, and US export controls on AI chips. The company says it is cooperating with BIS subpoenas and "has not been informed that we are the target" [2].
- **Circle of competence: 2/5.** To learn: customer concentration, Nvidia dependence, how much of the $12.9B inventory and $6.1B receivables is firm, the $34.2B of purchase commitments [3], and the Note 15 legal detail. I could not read the 10-K's cash-flow, controls and legal notes directly (the filing is very long); figures come from a 10-K excerpt and a third-party summary.

## L · Love (moat and management)
- **Moat:** none proven. The closest is speed to market with new Nvidia platforms and modular designs. Replication test: Dell, HPE and ODMs ship the same GPUs, so a rival with this market cap could build it. Disappears test: customers would switch to Dell or an ODM. Pricing power: gross margin 10.8% in FY2026 vs 11.1% in FY2025, a Q4 spike to 17.5%, and 12.0–12.5% indicated for Q1 FY27 [4], so margins swing by several points a quarter. `history_usd` ROIC: 7–10% in 2016–21, then 18%, 31%, 21%, 17%, 15%. High for only five years, and falling since 2023.
- **Management:** the allocation record is raising money, not returning it. Shares 518M (2016) → 697M (2026) in the fact pack, +5.4%/yr over 5 years; the 10-K cover shows 657.0M at July 2026 [2] (the gap is unreconciled). Debt: about $8.7B at 2026-06-30 ($2.0B JPMorgan revolver, $1.76B CTBC facilities, $0.7B 2028, $1.725B 2029 and $2.3B 2030 convertible notes), plus a mandatory convertible preferred [2]. **The screen shows only $134M of debt, so the screen's debt is wrong; its net debt of −$7.4B (net cash) is also unconfirmed and I do not rely on it.** FY2026 OCF was −$6.8B, funded by $9.5B of financing [3]. The OCF canary (METHOD 5) is dead. Controls: reported not effective as of 2026-06-30, with remediation in a second year [3][5]. A 2024 special committee advised hiring a new finance chief after process lapses [5]. The CFO left on 2026-10-15 with no stated reason; the new CFO Mark Long was Western Digital's CFO 2016–19 [5]. Co-founder Wally Liaw and others were indicted in March over an alleged AI-chip smuggling scheme; the company was not named and began its own investigation in April [5]. Proxy and incentives: not read. Insider trading: not checked.
- **Values:** the export-control matter is a legal and ethical item for the owner to weigh; I state only the facts above.
- Would I buy the whole company at this price? **No.** About $23B of market cap for a low-margin assembler that burns cash, with controls that are not effective.

## S · Story
- **Thesis:** if AI server demand holds, Supermicro takes a rising share of a very large market and earns 10–12% gross margin on enormous revenue, so earnings compound and 10x earnings is cheap. The bull case needs cash to follow earnings and governance to be clean.
- **Three things that must stay true:** (1) operating cash flow turns positive and tracks net income as working capital stabilises; (2) controls are remediated and the export-control inquiries end without an adverse finding; (3) gross margin holds at or above ~10% without new debt or equity every year.
- **Inversion:** the bear says this is a low-margin assembler of Nvidia's product, financing its customers through inventory and receivables, with a history of accounting problems and an indicted co-founder. A fall in AI capex would leave $12.9B of inventory and $34.2B of commitments. Rebuttal: the revenue beats are real. But I concede the debt and cash points; the rebuttal fails on them.
- **Sell triggers (if ever owned):** OCF still negative for the next four quarters; total debt above 3× FCF; gross margin below 9%; an adverse BIS/DOJ finding naming the company; an auditor resignation or restatement; another senior finance departure.
